# Who we help

We help **people doing good or genuinely useful work who are locked out of software because they can't afford it.** That's the whole test. This page makes it concrete so volunteers and partners both know where the line is.

We're free labor with a purpose — not free labor for anyone who asks. A clear, fair filter is what keeps the project sustainable and keeps volunteers motivated.

## Who's a fit

**Mission-driven organizations**
- Registered charities and nonprofits
- Community groups, mutual aid, clubs, and grassroots initiatives
- Social enterprises where profit clearly serves the mission

**Small local businesses** — when they meet the spirit below
- Family-run or owner-operated, no in-house engineers
- **Being for-profit is completely fine.** A neighborhood café or a sole trader earning a living is exactly who we mean.
- Small enough that paying market rate for software isn't realistic
- Rooted in and serving a local community (the café, the corner shop, the one-person trade)

## The fairness test for businesses

We're happy to help for-profit businesses — the filter isn't "do you make money," it's "can you afford this any other way." Helping for free only makes sense when it wouldn't otherwise happen. Ask:

1. **Can't reasonably pay.** Hiring a developer or agency is genuinely out of reach — not just something they'd rather not spend on.
2. **No engineering capacity.** No technical staff, no one who could do this themselves.
3. **Real community value.** A local institution, a livelihood, jobs, something a neighborhood would miss.
4. **Proportionate ask.** A first website, a booking form, an automation — not a venture-scale product or something they could monetize at scale off our backs.

A useful gut check: *if we don't help, does this just not happen?* If yes, it's likely a fit.

## Who's not a fit (for now)

- Well-funded organizations that can comfortably pay for this
- Businesses large enough to hire, already with technical staff, or backed by investors
- Work whose main payoff is scaling someone's wealth rather than sustaining a livelihood or serving a community
- Anything illegal, harmful, deceptive, or discriminatory
- Projects needing ongoing paid infrastructure the org can't sustain after we leave
- Open-ended commitments we can't responsibly staff with volunteers

## Edge cases

When it's genuinely unclear, raise it openly (an issue, or with maintainers) and decide together. Document the call in the client README so the reasoning is visible. Defaulting to "help" is fine for small asks; the bigger the ask, the more the fairness test matters.

> This is a starting point, not a rulebook. As FDE for Good matures (see [becoming-a-nonprofit.md](becoming-a-nonprofit.md)), formal eligibility criteria may become part of our governance.
