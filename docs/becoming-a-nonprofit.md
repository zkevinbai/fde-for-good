# Becoming a nonprofit

FDE for Good starts as a volunteer collective. The goal is to become a **registered nonprofit / charity in its own right** — so the work is sustainable, donations are possible, and volunteers and partners are properly backed.

This is the roadmap. It's deliberately staged: each step unlocks the next, and we only take a step when we've outgrown the one before it. Nothing here is legal advice — when the time comes we'll get proper guidance for our jurisdiction.

## Why incorporate at all

- **Take donations & grants** to cover hosting, tools, and eventually stipends.
- **Limit personal liability** for volunteers and organizers.
- **Be credible** to partners, funders, and other nonprofits.
- **Continuity** — outlast any single volunteer.
- **Hold assets** (domains, accounts, funds) in the org's name, not a person's.

## Stages

### Stage 0 — Volunteer collective *(we are here)*
- Do real engagements and build a track record.
- Establish principles, eligibility, and governance (this repo).
- Keep a simple, public record of who we've helped.
- **Exit criteria:** a handful of completed engagements and a small, committed core team.

### Stage 1 — Identity & shared resources
- Pick a name and secure the domain, email, and accounts (owned by the collective, documented in `governance.md`).
- Decide on a target jurisdiction (where the core team is) and the likely legal form.
- Draft a one-page mission / charitable purpose.
- **Exit criteria:** consistent identity and 2+ people willing to be organizers.

### Stage 2 — Fiscal sponsorship *(take money before incorporating)*
- Partner with an existing nonprofit or platform (e.g. Open Collective / a fiscal host) to receive donations and pay expenses **without** standing up a legal entity yet.
- Start covering real costs (hosting, domains) transparently.
- **Exit criteria:** money flows cleanly and transparently; demand justifies a real entity.

### Stage 3 — Incorporate
- Form the legal entity (e.g. nonprofit corporation / CIC / charity, depending on jurisdiction).
- Recruit an initial **board / trustees**; adopt bylaws and a conflict-of-interest policy.
- Appoint a treasurer; open a bank account in the org's name.
- **Exit criteria:** registered entity with a functioning board and books.

### Stage 4 — Tax-exempt / charitable status
- Apply for the relevant status (e.g. 501(c)(3) in the US, registered charity in the UK, etc.).
- Put basic compliance in place: record-keeping, annual reporting, donor receipts.
- **Exit criteria:** recognized status; able to offer tax-deductible giving where applicable.

### Stage 5 — Sustainability
- Diversify funding (grants, recurring donors, corporate matching/volunteering).
- Consider stipends or part-time roles for coordination so the work doesn't burn people out.
- Keep the open-source, embedded, free-to-partners model intact as we scale.

## Open questions to resolve before Stage 3

- **Jurisdiction** — where are the core organizers, and what legal form fits?
- **Charitable purpose** — does "helping small businesses" qualify as charitable everywhere, or do we frame it around community economic development / digital inclusion?
- **Boundary** — how do we keep helping small businesses without becoming a free dev shop? (See [who-we-help.md](who-we-help.md).)
- **IP & open source** — codify that engagements are MIT and partner data stays private.

## How to help with this

This is as much a project as the engineering. If you have nonprofit, legal, accounting, or fundraising experience, that's gold — open an issue and say hi.
